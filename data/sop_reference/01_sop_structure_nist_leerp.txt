SECTION 1: MANAGEMENT COMMITMENT (NIST SP 800-61r3 §2.1, LEERP §3.1)
=============================================================================

A cyber incident response policy MUST include a formal management commitment statement. This section:
- States that senior leadership mandates compliance with the policy
- Allocates necessary human, technical, and financial resources for incident response
- Authorises the Cyber Incident Response Team (CIRT) capability
- Designates the issuing authority responsible for policy approval and signature
- Confirms the policy is aligned with organisational risk management strategy

The management commitment section closes with: "This policy requires formal approval and signature from [issuing authority role and name]."

Management commitment is mandatory per NIST SP 800-61r3 §2.1: "Management must support the incident response capability by establishing a policy and ensuring that adequate resources are available."

SECTION 2: PURPOSE AND SCOPE (NIST SP 800-61r3 §2.3, LEERP §2)
=============================================================================

2.1 Purpose
The purpose section defines:
- The specific threat type or category this policy governs
- The operational objective (detection, containment, investigation, recovery)
- Why this policy is a priority for law enforcement agencies specifically

2.2 Scope
The scope section defines who and what the policy applies to:
- All ICT systems operated by or on behalf of the organisation
- All staff with access to operational networks, case management systems, criminal intelligence databases
- Third-party contractors and service providers with network or system access
- Geographic and organisational boundaries

2.3 Regulatory Framework — MANDATORY REFERENCES
Every LEA cyber incident response policy MUST explicitly reference the following legal instruments:

EU NIS2 DIRECTIVE 2022/2555 (Article 23):
- Mandatory incident reporting for essential entities in the public sector
- Early warning to national CERT/CSIRT within 24 hours of becoming aware of significant incident
- Incident notification within 72 hours
- Final incident report within one month

EU GENERAL DATA PROTECTION REGULATION (GDPR) 2016/679:
- Article 33: Personal data breach notification to supervisory authority without undue delay and within 72 hours of becoming aware
- Article 34: Communication of personal data breach to data subject without undue delay when high risk to rights and freedoms

BUDAPEST CONVENTION ON CYBERCRIME (ETS 185):
- Article 2: Illegal access to computer systems
- Article 5: System interference
- Article 6: Misuse of devices
- Article 11: Attempt and aiding or abetting
- Provides framework for international cooperation in cybercrime investigations

EU LAW ENFORCEMENT DIRECTIVE (LED) 2016/680:
- Data protection standards specific to criminal justice processing
- Applies to processing of personal data by competent authorities for prevention, investigation, detection or prosecution of criminal offences

SECTION 3: INCIDENT CLASSIFICATION (LEERP §4, Europol Common Taxonomy)
=============================================================================

Incidents must be classified using the Europol Common Taxonomy for Law Enforcement Agencies and CSIRTs. Classification consists of:
- Class (top-level category, scenario-level)
- Type (specific incident type, incident-level)
- Legislative mapping (applicable Budapest Convention articles and EU Directives)

Classification enables:
- Cross-border information sharing with other EU Member State LEAs via SIENA
- Consistent reporting to Europol EC3 under the EMPACT framework
- Correct application of applicable legal frameworks

SECTION 4: THREAT ASSESSMENT (NIST SP 800-61r3 §3.2, LEERP §4)
=============================================================================

4.1 Threat Profile
Must include:
- Threat actor category (organised criminal group, state-affiliated, insider threat, opportunistic)
- Initial attack vector (phishing, exploitation of vulnerability, supply chain, insider)
- Target assets (case management databases, criminal intelligence systems, communication infrastructure)
- Identified vulnerability class (unpatched systems, lack of MFA, network segmentation failure)
- Estimated operational impact if uncontained (operational disruption duration, data exposure risk, reputational damage)
- Likelihood assessment: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW

4.2 Simulation Evidence
When simulation data is available:
- Number of simulation runs conducted
- Average trainee performance score
- Most frequently missed expected actions with failure rates
- Per-phase performance breakdown
- Identification of capability gaps that translate to policy requirements

When no simulation data is available: "No simulation data available — assessment is threat-profile-based only."

SECTION 5: MANDATORY RESPONSE PROCEDURES (NIST SP 800-61r3 §3, LEERP §5)
=============================================================================

5.1 Detection and Initial Assessment (T+0 to T+30 minutes)
The first response phase covers:
- Recognition of indicators of compromise (IoC) specific to the threat type
- Initial system isolation WITHOUT powering off (to preserve volatile memory)
- Documentation of time of discovery, affected systems, and observable indicators
- Immediate notification via out-of-band communication channel
- Do NOT: attempt decryption, pay ransom, restart systems, or use potentially compromised communication channels

NIST SP 800-61r3 §3.2: "The most critical phase of incident response is detection and initial analysis. Performing initial analysis as quickly as possible is essential for determining the scope of the incident."

5.2 Containment (T+30 minutes to T+2 hours)
- Activate the Cyber Incident Response Team (CIRT)
- Implement network segmentation to prevent lateral movement
- Block inter-VLAN routing between operational and administrative segments
- Disable VPN access for non-essential personnel
- Review and revoke recently created or modified privileged accounts
- Verify backup integrity before any recovery decision
- Activate out-of-band communications for all incident coordination

NIST SP 800-61r3 §3.3: "Containment is important before an incident overwhelms resources or increases damage."

5.3 Evidence Preservation — MUST PRECEDE ANY REMEDIATION
CRITICAL: All evidence preservation steps MUST be completed BEFORE any remediation, patching, or system restoration.

Mandatory evidence preservation steps:
1. Create a verified forensic disk image of ALL affected systems using a hardware write-blocker
2. Record the SHA-256 cryptographic hash of each forensic image immediately after acquisition
3. Store images on isolated, write-protected, tamper-evident media
4. Acquire volatile memory (RAM) dumps from all running affected systems before any system is powered down
5. Collect and preserve all relevant log files in original, unmodified format:
   - System event logs (Windows Event Log: System, Security, Application)
   - Firewall, proxy, and IDS/IPS logs covering minimum 72-hour window prior to detection
   - VPN and remote access authentication logs
   - Email server logs (for phishing vector investigation)
   - Active Directory authentication and privilege escalation logs
6. Maintain a chain of custody log for every collected evidence item:
   - Who collected it, when, from which system, where stored
   - This log is legally required for criminal prosecution admissibility

NIST SP 800-61r3 §3.3: "Evidence collection should be done carefully to preserve the integrity of the data."
Legal requirement: Chain of custody documentation is required for evidence to be admissible in criminal proceedings under most EU member state procedural law.

5.4 Escalation Chain (LEERP §5.2, NIS2 Art.23)
A minimum of 6 escalation levels is required for LEA cyber incident response:

L1 — First Responder (Internal — Immediate)
Trigger: Any indicator of incident detected
Action: Isolate, document, and notify Duty ICT Officer immediately
Timeframe: Immediately upon detection

L2 — Incident Commander (Internal — Within 30 minutes)
Trigger: Incident confirmed on one or more systems
Action: Activate CIRT, initiate network isolation measures
Role: [LEA INPUT NEEDED — e.g. Head of IT Security / CISO / ICT Coordinator]

L3 — Senior Management (Internal — Within 1 hour)
Trigger: Operational systems affected or sensitive data potentially compromised
Action: Authorise significant containment decisions, manage external communications
Role: [LEA INPUT NEEDED — e.g. Head of Division / Deputy Commissioner]

L4 — Data Protection Officer (Internal — Within 1 hour)
Trigger: Personal data breach confirmed or suspected
Action: Begin data breach assessment, prepare GDPR Art.33 notification if required
Deadline: Internal notification within 1 hour; external notification to DPA within 72 hours (GDPR Art.33)

L5 — National CERT/CSIRT (External — Within 24 hours)
Trigger: Significant incident per NIS2 Art. 23 criteria
Action: Submit early warning notification to national CERT/CSIRT
Legal basis: NIS2 Directive 2022/2555, Article 23
Deadline: Early warning within 24 hours; full incident notification within 72 hours

L6 — Europol EC3 / LEERP Activation (External — As soon as feasible)
Trigger: Criminal infrastructure involved, cross-border impact suspected, or LEERP threshold reached
Action: Notify Europol EC3 through established SIENA channel; activate LEERP coordination if cross-border
Legal basis: LEERP (Council of EU ST-6527-2025), Europol Regulation 2016/794

LEERP Activation Thresholds (from EU Law Enforcement Emergency Response Protocol):
- Major cross-border cyber incident affecting two or more EU Member States
- Incident targeting critical infrastructure with cross-border significance
- Involvement of criminal groups operating across multiple jurisdictions

5.5 External Reporting Obligations
Mandatory reporting rows that every LEA policy MUST include:

Row 1: National Data Protection Authority
Legal basis: GDPR Article 33
Obligation: Personal data breach notification
Deadline: Within 72 hours of becoming aware (GDPR Art.33 is an absolute deadline — no exceptions)

SECTION 6: PREVENTIVE CONTROLS (NIST SP 800-61r3 §2.2, LEERP §6)
=============================================================================

6.1 Technical Controls
Technical controls must be grounded in the specific vulnerability class and simulation gaps identified:

Priority classification:
- CRITICAL: Controls that directly address the identified primary attack vector
- HIGH: Controls that address identified secondary vulnerability classes
- MEDIUM: Controls that improve overall security posture

Common mandatory technical controls for LEA environments:
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on ALL remote access (VPN, webmail, administrative interfaces)
- Network segmentation between operational, administrative, and internet-facing segments
- Offline or air-gapped backups tested quarterly
- Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) on all workstations and servers
- Patch management: critical CVEs remediated within 48 hours, high CVEs within 7 days
- Email filtering with attachment sandboxing and URL detonation
- Privileged Access Management (PAM) for administrative accounts
- Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) with 24/7 monitoring capability

6.2 Administrative Controls
- Mandatory annual cybersecurity awareness training for all personnel
- Bi-annual tabletop incident response exercises for CIRT (minimum)
- Annual penetration testing of all internet-facing systems and remote access infrastructure
- Third-party and contractor security assessment prior to any network access
- Periodic review of user access rights — remove inactive accounts and excess privileges

SECTION 7: ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES (NIST SP 800-61r3 §2.4, LEERP §3.2)
=============================================================================

Minimum required roles for LEA cyber incident response:

First Responder (any staff member):
- Responsibilities: Detect, document initial indicators, isolate the affected device, immediately notify the Duty ICT Officer. Do NOT take any other action.
- lea_input_needed: false (universal role)

Duty ICT Officer:
- Responsibilities: Receive notification, confirm incident, initiate initial network isolation measures, activate the CIRT
- lea_input_needed: false (role type is universal, title may vary)

Incident Commander:
- Responsibilities: Overall incident command and coordination. Final authority on containment and recovery decisions. Single point of contact for senior management.
- lea_input_needed: TRUE — validate rank and title against organisation's command structure

Digital Forensics Officer:
- Responsibilities: Lead all evidence acquisition and preservation activities per evidence preservation section. Maintain chain of custody. Liaison with prosecutorial authority on evidence admissibility.
- lea_input_needed: false (critical mandatory role)

Communications Officer / Public Affairs:
- Responsibilities: Manage all internal communications, coordinate external reporting obligations per external reporting section, handle media inquiries if applicable
- lea_input_needed: TRUE — may be a separate role or combined with another

Data Protection Officer (DPO):
- Responsibilities: Assess personal data breach impact, prepare GDPR Art.33 notification, advise on LED 2016/680 compliance, maintain data breach register
- lea_input_needed: TRUE — confirm role title and whether DPO is internal or external

Senior Management:
- Responsibilities: Authorise significant containment decisions (e.g. taking operational systems offline). Manage external stakeholder communications including ministerial notification.
- lea_input_needed: TRUE — validate rank/title against organisation's command structure

External Coordination Lead:
- Responsibilities: Liaise with national CERT/CSIRT, Europol EC3, and other external bodies under LEERP
- lea_input_needed: TRUE — confirm whether this is a separate role or combined with another
