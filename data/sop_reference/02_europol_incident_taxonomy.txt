DOCUMENT: Europol Common Taxonomy for Law Enforcement Agencies and CSIRTs — Incident Classification Reference
SOURCE: Europol EC3 / ENISA Common Taxonomy for Law Enforcement and CSIRTs (used in EMPACT framework)
PURPOSE: Authoritative incident classification reference for SAFE SOP generator

=============================================================================
OVERVIEW
=============================================================================

The Europol Common Taxonomy provides a standardised classification system used across EU Law Enforcement Agencies and Computer Security Incident Response Teams (CSIRTs). It enables:
- Consistent cross-border incident reporting via SIENA (Secure Information Exchange Network Application)
- Harmonised data collection for threat landscape analysis (IOCTA reports)
- Correct legislative mapping for criminal prosecution and civil obligations
- Interoperability between national LEAs and Europol EC3

Taxonomy Structure:
  Class (scenario-level) → Type (incident-level) → Sub-type (optional, tool/technique level)

Each incident reported to Europol EC3 must include:
- Primary Class and Type
- Legislative mapping (Budapest Convention article + applicable EU Directive)
- Incident severity (critical / high / medium / low)

=============================================================================
TAXONOMY CLASSES, TYPES, AND LEGISLATIVE MAPPINGS
=============================================================================

--- CLASS: MALWARE ---
Description: Any malicious software designed to disrupt, damage, or gain unauthorised access to a computer system.
Scenario-level label: "Malware incident"

Types within Malware class:
  - Ransomware: Encrypts victim data and demands ransom for decryption key. Variants: double extortion (encrypt + exfiltrate), triple extortion (encrypt + exfiltrate + DDoS).
  - Worm: Self-replicating malware that spreads across networks without user interaction.
  - Trojan / RAT (Remote Access Trojan): Disguised as legitimate software; provides attacker with remote control.
  - Spyware / Keylogger: Captures user credentials, keystrokes, or sensitive data.
  - Botnet agent: Infected system enrolled in attacker-controlled botnet.
  - Infostealer: Designed to harvest credentials, browser data, and sensitive files.
  - Malicious Command and Control (C&C): Malware communicating with attacker-controlled infrastructure.
  - Rootkit: Hides malware presence from operating system and security tools.
  - Cryptominer: Hijacks system resources for cryptocurrency mining.

Legislative mapping for Malware:
  - Budapest Convention Art. 2 (illegal access)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 4 (data interference — for ransomware specifically)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 5 (system interference)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 6 (misuse of devices — tools used to deploy malware)
  - NIS2 Directive Art. 3 (essential entity obligations), Art. 23 (incident reporting)
  - EU GDPR Art. 33-34 (if personal data is encrypted or exfiltrated)

--- CLASS: AVAILABILITY ---
Description: Attacks that disrupt, degrade, or destroy the availability of IT systems or data.
Scenario-level label: "Availability incident"

Types within Availability class:
  - Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS): Overwhelming a system or network with traffic to deny legitimate access. Sub-types: volumetric, protocol, application-layer.
  - Denial of Service (DoS): Single-source attack to deny availability.
  - Sabotage: Deliberate physical or logical destruction of IT systems.
  - Power disruption: Physical attack on power infrastructure affecting IT.
  - Communication disruption: Disruption of communication channels (radio, internet, telephone).
  - Ransomware-induced availability loss: Availability impact as consequence of ransomware encryption.

Legislative mapping for Availability:
  - Budapest Convention Art. 5 (system interference — primary)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 2 (illegal access — if combined with intrusion)
  - NIS2 Directive Art. 3, 23 (availability incidents qualify as significant incidents for essential entities)
  - EU Critical Entities Resilience Directive (CER) 2022/2557 (if critical infrastructure is targeted)

--- CLASS: INFORMATION GATHERING ---
Description: Attacks aimed at collecting information about target systems, networks, or persons without necessarily causing immediate damage.
Scenario-level label: "Information gathering incident"

Types within Information Gathering class:
  - Scanning / probing: Automated scanning of IP ranges, port scanning, vulnerability scanning.
  - Sniffing: Passive interception of network traffic to capture credentials or data.
  - Social engineering: Manipulating individuals to disclose information or perform actions (not phishing-specific).
  - Phishing: Deceptive communications (email, SMS, voice) to obtain credentials or install malware.
  - Spear-phishing: Targeted phishing directed at specific individuals (high-value targets in LEAs).
  - Whaling: Spear-phishing targeting senior executives or command officers.
  - Vishing / Smishing: Voice or SMS-based phishing.
  - Open Source Intelligence (OSINT) abuse: Exploitation of publicly available information for attack planning.
  - DNS enumeration: Mapping of organisation's DNS infrastructure.

Legislative mapping for Information Gathering:
  - GDPR Art. 5 (data minimisation principle violated by unauthorised collection), Art. 25 (data protection by design)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 2 (illegal access — when scanning leads to system access)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 6 (misuse of devices — scanning tools)
  - EU Directive on attacks against information systems 2013/40/EU, Art. 6 (illegal interception)

--- CLASS: INTRUSION ATTEMPT ---
Description: Attempted but not necessarily successful unauthorised access to systems.
Scenario-level label: "Intrusion attempt incident"

Types within Intrusion Attempt class:
  - Exploitation of known vulnerability: Attempted exploitation of CVE-listed vulnerability.
  - Exploitation of unknown vulnerability (zero-day): Attempted exploitation of previously unknown vulnerability.
  - Brute force attack: Systematic password guessing or credential stuffing.
  - Password spraying: Low-volume password attempts across many accounts to avoid lockout.
  - Credential stuffing: Using leaked credential lists to attempt authentication.
  - SQL injection attempt: Attempted database manipulation via malformed input.
  - Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) attempt: Attempted injection of malicious scripts.
  - API abuse: Attempted exploitation of application programming interfaces.

Legislative mapping for Intrusion Attempt:
  - Budapest Convention Art. 2 (illegal access — attempt provision under Art. 11)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 6 (misuse of devices — tools used for intrusion attempts)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 11 (attempt and aiding or abetting)
  - NIS2 Directive Art. 3, 23 (near-miss incidents should be assessed for significant incident threshold)

--- CLASS: INTRUSION ---
Description: Successful unauthorised access to systems or data.
Scenario-level label: "Intrusion incident"

Types within Intrusion class:
  - Account compromise: Attacker has gained control of a legitimate user or service account.
  - Privileged account compromise: Attacker has gained administrative or privileged access.
  - Lateral movement: Attacker moves from initial point of compromise to other systems.
  - SQL injection (successful): Successful database manipulation via malformed input.
  - Unauthorised physical access: Physical access to secure systems or data centres.
  - Supply chain compromise: Intrusion via third-party software, hardware, or service provider.
  - Insider threat: Authorised user abuses access for malicious purposes.
  - Web application compromise: Successful exploitation of web-facing application.
  - Remote code execution: Attacker executes code on victim system.

Legislative mapping for Intrusion:
  - Budapest Convention Art. 2 (illegal access — primary for all intrusion types)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 7 (computer-related forgery — if credentials were forged)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 8 (computer-related fraud — if financial gain is motive)
  - NIS2 Directive Art. 3, 23 (significant incident reporting mandatory)
  - EU GDPR Art. 33-34 (if personal data accessed — mandatory notification)
  - EU Directive 2013/40/EU Art. 3 (illegal access to information systems)

--- CLASS: INFORMATION CONTENT SECURITY ---
Description: Incidents involving the unauthorised access to, theft of, or manipulation of information.
Scenario-level label: "Information security incident"

Types within Information Content Security class:
  - Unauthorised data access: Accessing data without authorisation, even if not exfiltrated.
  - Data exfiltration: Unauthorised transfer of data outside the organisation.
  - Data manipulation / tampering: Unauthorised modification of data to corrupt or falsify.
  - Data destruction: Deliberate deletion or destruction of data.
  - Sensitive data exposure: Unintentional exposure of sensitive data (misconfiguration, etc.).
  - Criminal intelligence database breach: Unauthorised access to law enforcement sensitive databases.
  - Operational data leak: Leakage of ongoing operations data.

Legislative mapping for Information Content Security:
  - EU GDPR Art. 5 (data integrity), Art. 33-34 (breach notification — mandatory for personal data)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 2 (illegal access)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 4 (data interference — for manipulation/destruction)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 7 (computer-related forgery — for data manipulation)
  - EU Law Enforcement Directive (LED) 2016/680 — applies specifically to law enforcement personal data processing

--- CLASS: FRAUD AND ABUSIVE CONTENT ---
Description: Incidents involving deception for financial or operational gain, and distribution of harmful content.
Scenario-level label: "Fraud / Abusive Content incident"

Types within Fraud class:
  - Identity theft: Theft and fraudulent use of personal identity information.
  - Payment fraud: Fraudulent manipulation of payment systems.
  - Business Email Compromise (BEC): Impersonation of trusted entities to initiate fraudulent transactions.
  - Advance fee fraud: Deceptive communications promising large sums to victims.
  - SPAM: Unsolicited bulk email, often carrying malware or phishing links.
  - Child sexual abuse material (CSAM): Requires immediate escalation to EC3 / national authority.
  - Online harassment / cyberstalking: Digital harassment targeting individuals.
  - Disinformation: Deliberate spread of false information targeting LEA operations or public trust.

Legislative mapping for Fraud:
  - Budapest Convention Art. 8 (computer-related fraud)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 7 (computer-related forgery — for identity documents)
  - EU eCommerce Directive 2000/31/EC (online service provider obligations)
  - Budapest Convention Art. 9 (offences related to child pornography)
  - EU GDPR Art. 33-34 (if personal data of victims is involved)
  - Europol mandate (Regulation 2016/794) — Europol specifically mandated for cybercrime and online fraud

=============================================================================
INCIDENT SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION
=============================================================================

Severity levels for use in policy documents and incident reporting:

CRITICAL:
- Active encryption or destruction of operational systems
- Confirmed personal data breach affecting large number of data subjects
- Cross-border impact confirmed or highly suspected
- Criminal intelligence database compromised
- Systems supporting active criminal investigations affected
Reporting requirement: Immediate escalation to all levels including Europol EC3 / LEERP

HIGH:
- Active intrusion with lateral movement in progress
- Ransomware deployed but not yet widely spread
- Personal data breach confirmed, smaller scale
- Operational disruption to non-critical systems
Reporting requirement: Escalate to L3-L5 within timeframes; assess for NIS2 significant incident

MEDIUM:
- Intrusion attempt detected and blocked
- Malware infection contained to single system
- Phishing campaign targeting staff (even if no credentials captured)
- Suspicious scanning or reconnaissance activity
Reporting requirement: Internal escalation L1-L2; assess for significant incident threshold

LOW:
- Automated vulnerability scan from external IP
- Single phishing email (blocked by filters)
- Isolated policy violation
Reporting requirement: Document and monitor; no external reporting required

=============================================================================
EMPACT FRAMEWORK INCIDENT CATEGORIES
=============================================================================

The EMPACT (European Multidisciplinary Platform Against Criminal Threats) framework organises cybercrime into priority areas that align with the Europol taxonomy:

EMPACT Cybercrime Priority Areas (relevant to LEA SOPs):
1. Ransomware / Malware attacks on critical infrastructure and essential services
2. Child Sexual Exploitation Online (CSEO)
3. Online fraud (payment card fraud, BEC, cryptocurrency fraud)
4. Dark web / Criminal marketplaces
5. Attacks on critical infrastructure (OT/ICS environments)

For LEA organisations: most incidents will fall under EMPACT Cybercrime area 1 (ransomware/malware on public sector) or area 3 (fraud targeting LEA financial systems).

=============================================================================
FORMAT FOR INCIDENT CLASSIFICATION IN SOP DOCUMENTS
=============================================================================

Standard format for the incident_classification section in SAFE-generated SOPs:

"Class: [Europol Taxonomy Class]
Type: [Europol Taxonomy Type(s)]
Sub-type (if applicable): [specific variant or technique]
EMPACT Priority Area: [relevant EMPACT category]
Legislative mapping:
  - Budapest Convention: [Article numbers and descriptions]
  - NIS2 Directive: [Applicable articles]
  - GDPR: [Applicable articles if personal data involved]
  - Other: [LED, CER Directive, etc. if applicable]
Severity: [CRITICAL / HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW]"

Example for Ransomware scenario:
"Class: Malware
Type: Ransomware (double extortion — encryption + data exfiltration)
EMPACT Priority Area: Ransomware / Malware attacks on critical infrastructure
Legislative mapping:
  - Budapest Convention Art. 2 (illegal access), Art. 4 (data interference), Art. 5 (system interference), Art. 6 (misuse of devices)
  - NIS2 Directive Art. 3 (essential entity obligations), Art. 23 (significant incident reporting)
  - EU GDPR Art. 33 (breach notification to supervisory authority within 72 hours), Art. 34 (notification to data subjects)
Severity: CRITICAL — operational systems affected; personal data potentially exfiltrated"
